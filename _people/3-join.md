## Join the lab


### Prospective postgraduate (masters or PhD) students

If you are interested in the research we are doing in my lab and would like to join, please get in touch! First check your eligibility by perusing the [UC eligibility requirements](https://www.canterbury.ac.nz/study/uc-graduate-school/future-research-students/become-a-research-student). If you are eligible and interested, please [email me](sarah.flanagan@canterbury.ac.nz) with your CV and a brief summary of your research interests. 

### Prospective internship students from other universities

Any visiting students need to enrol in a course at the University of Canterbury, [BIOL307](https://courseinfo.canterbury.ac.nz/GetCourseDetails.aspx?course=BIOL307), which does incur international student fees. Enrolling in the course does not require formal coursework, as your internship visit is the expected coursework. Please [contact me](sarah.flanagan@canterbury.ac.nz) with information on your degree programme, internship duration and requirements, and your research interests. 

### UC students considering an honours or masters

If you are currently at University of Canterbury and would like to work with me for your Honours or Masters research, please [email me](sarah.flanagan@canterbury.ac.nz) with your CV and a brief description of your research interests.

### Diversity and Equity

My lab is inclusive, welcoming, and committed to advancing diversity; I welcome everyone regardless of their citizenship, ethnicity, color, socioeconomic background, gender identity, sexual orientation, religion, and age. I especially encourage members of historically underrepresented groups including (but not limited to) people of colour, women, veterans, and people from socioeconomically disadvantaged communities to inquire about opportunities and apply to join my group.


For more information on pursuing a postgraduate degree at the University of Canterbury, please take a look at the [handbook](http://www.canterbury.ac.nz/science/schools-and-departments/biological-sciences/postgraduate-study/).

### A word of advice

Sarah receives many expressions of interest to work with her, most of which are generic and seem to be drafted by an Artificial Intelligence LLM. These types of emails are not well-received, as they do not convey the things that distinguish you from other applicants. The strongest expressions of interest convey your passion or motivation for the research questions, identify why the Flanagan lab **in particular** is appealing to you, and outline specific details about the type(s) of research experience(s) you are hoping to achieve in the Flanagan Lab. [This blog post](https://blekhman.substack.com/p/why-i-am-not-reading-your-ai-generated) describes in detail some of the reasons that an AI-generated email are not particularly effective.
